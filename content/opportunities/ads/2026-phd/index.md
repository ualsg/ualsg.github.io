### Positions and research agenda

The [Urban Analytics Lab](/about/), a research group at the [National University of Singapore (NUS)](http://www.nus.edu.sg), is seeking two PhD researchers in urban data science.
These are fully funded PhD positions for four years during which the successful candidates are expected to fulfil the requirements for a PhD degree at NUS.

The PhD researchers will develop their research line and grow into independent scholars. 

They will also contribute to teaching activities and various service tasks in the research group and department such as co-supervision of master students, assisting with the preparation of proposals, and managing resources and visits.

There are two PhD positions available, one in each of the following broad areas, within which the candidate will develop their own focused PhD project:

1. _Sensing urban changes and dynamics with emerging urban data (PhD-1)._ Cities change daily and continuously. The established instruments for observing change, predominantly remote sensing, view cities from above and at intervals, but much urban change is subtle and invisible from a top-down view, happens between acquisitions, or leaves no substantial physical trace. This PhD will develop new urban representations, methods and instruments for urban change detection, capturing the dynamics that conventional techniques miss, from time-series street-level imagery to continuous crowdsourced data streams such as place reviews. Building on the detected changes, the project may also study their causes and effects, e.g. drivers such as climate and economic conditions, what they lead to in turn, or whether planning interventions actually work, with the exact focus shaped by the candidate's interests. This position suits applicants with strong foundations in computer science, geoinformatics and/or urban planning, some background in statistics, and curiosity about how cities change and adapt.

2. _Trustworthy Urban AI: quality, bias and uncertainty (PhD-2)._ AI is being deployed rapidly everywhere, but can we trust the data and models that underpin urban analytics? Cities are increasingly measured, modelled and managed by AI built on multimodal urban data (e.g. imagery, text, sensor streams), much of it of unknown quality and, in the generative-AI era, of potentially eroding reliability and authenticity. This PhD will advance the science of reliability for Urban AI. Examples include developing methods to estimate the completeness and bias of urban datasets without ground truth; tracing how uncertainty propagates from urban data through models to indicators and decisions, and how it varies across space; and benchmarking Urban AI systems -- including geospatial foundation models -- for robustness to data contamination, hallucination, and poor transferability across cities. The project may also go beyond diagnosis, for instance by examining whether AI agents can monitor and improve the quality of urban data. This position suits applicants with strong quantitative, statistical or machine learning foundations.

The topics above are starting points and broader frames.
The researchers will be expected to develop their focused line of research based on discussions with the advisor and other members of the research group during the first stage of their PhD.

The PhD students will be enrolled in the PhD programme of our [Department of Architecture](https://cde.nus.edu.sg/arch/) at the [College of Design and Engineering](http://www.cde.nus.edu.sg).
They will be supervised by the Principal Investigator of the Lab -- [Prof Filip Biljecki](/author/filip-biljecki/), and will collaborate with other members of the research group.

Candidates should have strong computational and research skills and a master's degree in computer science, geographical information science, urban planning, geomatics, engineering, statistics, or another relevant field of study, with evidence of research aptitude.

The Lab and Department provide a motivating, conducive, and flexible environment that enables researchers to explore their interests and curiosity, and build skills required for future career steps.
Our current and former members, including [PhD graduates](/doctors), have [published](/publication) dozens of impactful papers in leading journals and have secured meaningful and significant placements in the continuation of their academic career, including faculty positions.
Their outputs and accomplishments transcend papers, and also include invited talks, community initiatives, and [open datasets and open-source software](/data-code/).

### Required qualifications

A strong interest in urban analytics / GIScience / GeoAI, supported by relevant technical and scientific skills, together with proactivity, initiative, ownership, and continuous learning aspirations, is key to these positions.
Please also check [this guide that we prepared (including our expectations from a PhD)](/opportunities/application-guide/).

You should demonstrate that you possess:

- A master's degree :mortar_board: in a related discipline from a reputable university. Current MSc candidates are eligible to apply as well, provided that they are in the final stage of their studies and that their degree will be conferred before the start of the PhD programme.
- Evidence of research aptitude, such as a publication, research internship / experience, and/or a compelling proposal.
- Ability to identify research gaps and conceptualise research plans and methods to address them.
- Proficiency in one or more of the following areas: urban data science, computer science, GIScience, geomatics, remote sensing (in the urban/built environment), computer vision / deep learning, urban planning, and/or urban simulations. We do not expect applicants to be proficient in all the topics we work on :unicorn:, however, we ask them to demonstrate that they have skills in at least one of the related topics, have affinity for expanding their expertise beyond their main focus, and have solid ideas about how to continue their research aspirations.
- Very good communication skills in English (oral and written).
- Strong computational skills and experience with programming and data science toolkits. We will prioritise applicants who can demonstrate these qualities through a GitHub profile or similar evidence. Further, proficiency with (i) general academic tools such as LaTeX; (ii) geospatial tools such as QGIS; (iii) plotting libraries such as ggplot or matplotlib; and (iv) statistics is a strong plus.
- An ability to work with others and in an international environment.
- Curiosity and passion to explore new concepts, methods and technologies, and capability of identifying and quickly learning the most suitable tools for the research on the go.
- Optional: a positive and proactive stance on open science, preferably with demonstrated activities such as using and developing open-source software, releasing data as open data, and promoting reproducible research.

### Duration and location

Each position is funded for four years.
The positions have two possible start dates: August 2027 and January 2028 (in alignment with the start of the semesters at NUS).

The PhD is based in Singapore.
This is not a remote position.

The PhD student will work in our research group.
We offer flexible working arrangements similar to most academic environments (e.g. no strict office hours and the possibility to spend a portion of time working from home).

Please apply by [email](mailto:AKIBX006@nus.edu.sg) (write "Application: PhD-1" in the subject for the first position, or "Application: PhD-2" for the second one), including the following:

* Academic CV
* Which intake you prefer (August 2027 or January 2028)
* A cover letter / statement (about one page) briefly indicating:
    * (i) your computational skills and relevant experience so far and how they would translate to the benefit of this PhD position, and your learning goals and aspirations;
    * (ii) outlook on one of the aforementioned topics with some ideas and how you plan to tackle such a problem; 
    * (iii) how you intend to contribute to our growing research group and enhance its activities.
    * Keep it short and simple, please. A few paragraphs for each of these points is sufficient.
* A PhD research proposal addressing either of the two topics (up to a few pages).
* Links to relevant materials such as a paper published in a reputable conference or journal that evidences your research aptitude, background and experience. Please do not provide group projects from your coursework or similar. Only material relevant to this position will be considered.
* Names and contact information for three referees. There is no need to submit recommendation letters at this stage. By providing this information, we assume that we have your and their permission to contact them to learn more about you. Letters will be required later from applicants selected for the positions.
* Other materials showing that you meet the [required qualifications](#required-qualifications) and aptitude for research, e.g. link to a public GitHub repository you have developed, recorded presentation, and portfolio. Candidates who can demonstrate strong computational skills and have research experience will be prioritised.

We appreciate your understanding that only applicants shortlisted for the interview will be notified.
The selected candidates will later be asked to provide the full documentation, e.g. academic certificates and transcripts, copy of ID, and three letters of recommendation; and go through the application to the PhD programme at NUS for official admission.
